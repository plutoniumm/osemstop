The browser only polls and plots. The loop controls velocity with setpoint zero, so e = −vel: the validated −0.030 is Kp, integrating gives a spring, differentiating gives added mass. What is real and what is modelled

Real, executed as-is: Controller, Channel, OnePoleFilter, SlidingRMS, slew_limit and RateLimitedActuator, imported from the controller file and stepped once per sample. The state machine, the gain schedule, the runaway and saturation breakers, the rail interlock, the lock detector and the auto-recovery are that code running, not a description of it. Edit the Python, restart the server, and this page changes.

Faked, and only this: serial is stubbed so pyDAC imports without a port — nothing in the stub is ever called — and DACController is replaced by a FakeDAC that validates its arguments identically and remembers the last voltage per channel. That held voltage is what the simulated coil pulls on, so the controller's own 10 ms throttle and 0.5 mV deadband shape the force exactly as on the bench.

The converter is modelled, and it is the one nonlinearity. Sensor volts are offset to each channel's measured resting count, rounded to an integer and hard-clipped to 0–1023; the controller then scales those counts back to volts itself, exactly as read_sample() does on the bench. The OSEMs rest well above mid-scale — measured 2026-08-03 at 615 / 641 / 717 / 679 counts against a mid-scale of 511.5 — so the headroom is asymmetric and large amplitude flat-tops the TOP rail long before it reaches the bottom one. That is what the bench logs show: 2.19% of samples railed on the v1 run, 10.40% on v2, none of them at zero.

Modelled, not measured: the plant — a second-order oscillator per axis at ~1 Hz with adjustable Q, driven by a coherent tone plus broadband noise. Each axis has its own mode frequency (within ±1%) and its own lever arm, which is why the traces do not sit on top of one another: the suspension is sharp enough (half-width f0/2Q = 1%) that under 1% of frequency spread throws them tens of degrees apart in phase. Coil authority, Q and drive are sliders because none were characterised on the bench.

The disturbances reach it by three different paths, which is the point of having them. The drive, the seismic noise and the HVAC rumble are forces on the mass, so the suspension's own transfer function decides what survives: measured here, one fixed force of 3.0 moves the optic 1567 mV at the 1 Hz resonance and 0.28 mV at 40 Hz. Mains pickup is not a force — it is added to the sensor line downstream of the optic, so the mass is genuinely still at 60 Hz while the controller sees motion and pushes against it. It survives the 0.4–3 Hz bandpass at about 1/20th of its amplitude, which is why 240 mV of hum — twelve times the measurement noise, unmissable on the unfiltered wave — moves the amplitude ratio from 0.254 to 0.258 and shows up only as extra jitter on the actuator. That is the bandpass doing its job. Shocks are velocity impulses to the whole rigid body: one bump, all four OSEMs, so what separates the traces afterwards is damping and nothing else.

What this will not reproduce: the instability and rail onset seen at Kp = −0.040. The plant is linear and delay-free apart from the actuator hold, so here more negative Kp is always more damping — push it to −0.6 and it just damps harder. Whatever turns −0.040 into instability on the real suspension is not in these equations. The sim cannot tell you a safe gain; it shows you the logic.

Two findings worth your attention. Tick Blind on a channel with measurement noise at or below 5 mV and the rail interlock freezes all four axes in about half a second, as designed. Raise the noise to 10 mV and it never fires at all: RAIL_LOW_COUNTS is 3 counts of 1023 (~14.7 mV) and the check needs 250 consecutive samples under it. The blind channel then reports an amplitude ratio near zero — steadier than any working axis — while the loop stays in DAMPING and declares itself locked. Separately: set drive amplitude to 0 and raise seismic noise, and the controller trips repeatedly with nothing wrong, because a 2 s RMS compared against one 8 s baseline cannot characterise a process that wanders on the ringdown timescale. Both are real defects, preserved on purpose in v0, v1 and v2 and fixed in v3 — run make sim V=v3 and neither reproduces. A third is not visible from this page: in v0–v2 a saturation trip latches FAULT permanently, because the flag that gates recovery is only recomputed in a branch FAULT never reaches. The harness asserts the broken behaviour for versions that do not claim the fix and the correct behaviour for those that do, so the difference is measured rather than asserted. See versions.md.
