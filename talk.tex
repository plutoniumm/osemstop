\begin{frame}{The plant: three resonances, and a bound on the damping}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.47\textwidth}
      \footnotesize
      A suspended optic is a damped harmonic oscillator. For one sensed
      coordinate $x(t)$, mass $m$, force $F(t)$, and mode $m$:
      \[ \ddot{x} + \frac{\omega_m}{Q_m}\,\dot{x} + \omega_m^2 x = \frac{F(t)}{m} \]
      \vspace{-3mm}

      Amplitude decays as $e^{-\gamma_m t}$, so
      \[ \gamma_m = \frac{\omega_m}{2 Q_m}, \qquad \tau_m \equiv 1/\gamma_m. \]

    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.51\textwidth}
      \textbf{Resonances}, 320\,s ringdown, coils at bias:
      \[ f_A, f_B, f_C = 0.7155,\ 0.9949,\ 1.6396\ \mathrm{Hz} \]
      \vspace{1mm}
      \begin{center}$\pm 0.0001$\,Hz\end{center}
      \footnotesize\raggedright
      $\gamma_m$ across channels, in s$^{-1}$:

      \vspace{1mm}
      \centering
      {\scriptsize
      \begin{tabular}{@{}lccc@{}}
        \toprule
        & ch & median & range \\
        \midrule
        A & 4 & 0.0483 & $0.0107$--$0.1388$ \\
        B & 5 & 0.0082 & $0.0005$--$0.0579$ \\
        C & 4 & 0.0094 & $0.0061$--$0.0530$ \\
        \bottomrule
      \end{tabular}}
      \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Three gains: P is a damper, I is a spring, D is a mass}
  \centering
  {\footnotesize A \textbf{PID controller} combines three responses to an error:
  one proportional to it, one to its running total, one to its rate of change.
  Here the ``error'' is the mirror's speed, which shifts what each term means.}

  \vspace{1mm}
  \[
    \underbrace{K_p\,e = -K_p\,\dot{x}}_{\text{force} \propto \text{velocity}}
    \qquad
    \underbrace{K_i\!\int\! e\,dt = -K_i\,x}_{\text{force} \propto \text{displacement}}
    \qquad
    \underbrace{K_d\,\frac{de}{dt} = -K_d\,\ddot{x}}_{\text{force} \propto \text{acceleration}}
  \]
  \vspace{1mm}
  {\footnotesize
  \begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
    \toprule
    term & acts on & physically & effect on the pole \\
    \midrule
    $K_p$ & velocity & \textbf{viscous damper} & \alert{\textbf{the only term that removes energy}} \\
    $K_i$ & displacement & added \textbf{spring} & moves the resonance; does not damp \\
    $K_d$ & acceleration & added (negative) \textbf{mass} & noisiest; differentiates twice \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}}

  \vspace{3mm}
  \begin{minipage}{0.9\textwidth}
  \footnotesize
  \textbf{Why add I and D at all?} $K_p$ sets the damping, but it leaves a steady
  offset it cannot remove and it reacts only to present error. $K_i$ takes out the
  offset; $K_d$ anticipates (and therefore useless for us)

  \end{minipage}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The gain is not one fixed number}
  \scriptsize
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.50\textwidth}
      \textbf{Three definitions, used for the rest of the deck.}
      \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
        \item \textbf{Envelope:} the running size of recent motion, an RMS of
              the bandpassed sensor signal. Window per job, 1 / 2 / 5\,s
              (\texttt{osem.delta.py}).
        \item \textbf{Baseline:} the envelope measured once per run with the
              gain forced to zero. This room, this night, nothing driving.
        \item $r$ = envelope now / that channel's own baseline. $r = 1$ is
              ``as lively as when we were not pushing''.
      \end{itemize}

      \vspace{1mm}
      \textbf{Why declare two gain levels at all.} $K_p$ is the strength of the
      artificial drag: volts onto the coil per volt-per-second of sensor speed.
      Large motion wants the stiffest drag the hardware allows, to be caught
      quickly. A mirror near rest does not: its sensor signal is mostly noise, so
      a stiff drag pumps that noise back into the coils, and the coil drive has
      the fixed $\pm 0.25$\,V window from the previous slide. Hence a
      \textbf{capture} level for large $r$, a \textbf{steady} level for small.

      \vspace{1mm}
      That is the argument in general. \textbf{No justification for \emph{this}
      rig having two levels is recorded anywhere in the project}: not in the
      source, not in \texttt{versions.md}, not in a docstring.
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \raggedright
      \alert{\textbf{One number, measured once, in one window, decides all three
      of these.}}
      {\footnotesize
      \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1.5pt}
        \item \textbf{The fault threshold.} The runaway breaker trips above
              $1.8 \times$ baseline.
        \item \textbf{The gain blend.} Full capture above $r = 0.6$, tapering to
              steady by $r = 0.2$.
        \item \textbf{The definition of success.} \texttt{LOCKED} means below
              $0.35 \times$ baseline, held 5\,s.
      \end{itemize}}

      \vspace{1mm}
      \centering
      \begin{tikzpicture}
        \begin{axis}[width=5.4cm, height=2.4cm,
                     xlabel={$r$}, ylabel={$|K_p|$ (V per V/s)},
                     scaled y ticks=false,
                     xmin=0, xmax=1.0, ymin=0.028, ymax=0.038,
                     axis lines=left, ytick={0.030,0.035},
                     yticklabels={$0.030$, $0.035$}]
          \addplot[gray!45, dashed, samples=200, domain=0:1]
            {0.030 + 0.005*max(0,min(1,(x-0.2)/0.4))};
          \addplot[very thick, samples=2, domain=0:1] {0.035};
        \end{axis}
      \end{tikzpicture}\\
      {\scriptsize Solid: what ships. Both levels are $\pm 0.035$, so the
      schedule is flat and the blend is inert. Grey dashed: the shape the code
      can express, never used. Still live: the rate limit, $0.02$ per second,
      and the soft-start it implies, $0.035/0.02 = 1.75$\,s from zero.}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Two consequences of a loop rate that moves}
  \footnotesize
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{1. One threshold was counted in samples, not seconds.}

      \vspace{1mm}
      \texttt{MAX\_CONSECUTIVE\_SATURATED = 30} trips after 30 consecutive
      samples with the coil command pinned at the end of its window. It is the
      only threshold in the codebase counted that way, so its real meaning moved
      with the coil count:

      \vspace{1.5mm}
      \centering
      {\scriptsize
      \begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
        \toprule
        coils driven & 1 & 4 & 5 & 8 \\
        loop rate (Hz) & 73.0 & 23.5 & 18.2 & 12.5 \\
        \midrule
        \textbf{30 samples (s)} & 0.41 & \textbf{1.28} & 1.65 & \textbf{2.40} \\
        \bottomrule
      \end{tabular}}\\[1mm]
      {\scriptsize Bottom row: what 30 consecutive samples amounts to in
      wall-clock seconds.}

      \vspace{1.5mm}
      \raggedright
      \alert{The interlock got \textbf{less} sensitive exactly as the rig got
      harder to control:} same 30 samples, six times longer on the wire.
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{2. Every gain in the rig was fixed on a 4-coil loop.}

      \vspace{1.5mm}
      A feedback loop always pushes a little late. \textbf{Phase margin} is how
      much more lateness it can absorb before the push lands \emph{with} the
      motion instead of against it, at which point the loop adds energy rather
      than removing it. Quoted in degrees of one cycle, at a stated frequency.

      \vspace{1.5mm}
      At \textbf{3.75\,Hz}: \textbf{73.5$^\circ$} on 4 coils,
      \textbf{45.5$^\circ$} on 8
      (\texttt{analysis/out/phase\_budget.csv}). Carrying the same gains onto
      eight coils spends \textbf{28$^\circ$} of a budget nobody re-measured.

      \vspace{1.5mm}
      {\scriptsize \textbf{Read 3.75\,Hz with care.} It is one of the fixed
      evaluation frequencies in that file, not one of the three measured modes
      (0.7155 / 0.9949 / 1.6396\,Hz), and \textbf{nothing in the project records
      why it was chosen}. Take the pair as a comparison of two configurations,
      not as a margin at a frequency that matters.}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Defects fixed without argument: the three \texttt{zero} shipped}
  \scriptsize
  \textbf{An \emph{interlock}} is an automatic check that pulls the loop out of
  \texttt{DAMPING} and parks the coils at bias; it \emph{arms} once it has enough
  evidence to fire. \textbf{The \emph{runaway breaker}} is the interlock watching
  for the loop feeding the motion instead of removing it.
  
  All three below were found in \texttt{zero} and answered in \texttt{alpha}.
  Each gets one line, because stating it is the same as explaining it.

  \vspace{1mm}
  \textbf{1. \texttt{saturation-latch}.}
  \emph{Intended:} count consecutive samples with the coil command pinned at a
  rail, fault above 30.
  \emph{Actual:} the flag was recomputed only inside \texttt{check\_runaway()},
  which does not run in \texttt{FAULT}, so once it tripped nothing cleared it: a
  permanent fault in a module advertising auto-recovery.
  \emph{Evidence:} across \textbf{16\,966} \texttt{FAULT} samples the run logged
  \alert{\textbf{one} distinct amplitude ratio, 3.6803}. It latched $\sim$50\,s.
  \emph{Fix:} refresh the flag every sample in every state; in \texttt{FAULT} the
  output sits at bias, so it clears itself. \texttt{alpha}, same bench: \textbf{0
  pinned samples, 0.00\% ADC clipped, no fault}.

  \vspace{1mm}
  \textbf{2. \texttt{rail-threshold}.}
  \emph{Intended:} call a channel blind when its raw counts stay under
  \texttt{RAIL\_LOW\_COUNTS} $=3$ of 1023 (14.7\,mV) for 250 consecutive samples.
  \emph{Actual:} a blind channel still carries about one count of dark noise, so
  one sample above 3 resets the counter and the interlock never arms.
  \emph{Evidence:} a four-channel bench run logged \textbf{246} genuinely clipped
  ADC samples, \textbf{2.19\%} of the run, and \textbf{the flag never set once}.
  \emph{Fix:} threshold $3 \to 12$ counts, and the test becomes 80\% of a 0.5\,s
  window rather than an unbroken run. Arms in \textbf{0.41\,s} at 0, 5, 10 and
  20\,mV of injected noise; the old test \textbf{never armed} at 10 or 20\,mV.

  \vspace{1mm}
  \textbf{3. \texttt{runaway-baseline}.}
  \emph{Intended:} one RMS over one 8\,s window at zero gain gives the baseline.
  \emph{Actual:} a bump inside that window inflates the baseline, and every
  threshold derived from it, for the whole run.
  \emph{Evidence:} three runs, same rig, same afternoon, ch0 baseline
  \textbf{0.6316 / 0.3158 / 0.1804\,V}, a \textbf{3.5$\times$} spread, while the
  absolute locked RMS was \textbf{0.139 / 0.128 / 0.115}. Equally well damped;
  only the yardstick moved.
  \emph{Fix, partial:} calibrate 20\,s, take the median of 5 sub-windows. Worst
  shock skew $39.3\% \to \mathbf{19.7\%}$ against a 35\% assertion line
  (simulator, 6\,V/s impulse swept across the window). \textbf{The code calls it
  partial}; defect 6 finishes it.
\end{frame}
