# What we actually know about `Kp = -0.040`

Analysis of the 27 bench logs in `data/` plus `bench/20260804/`, 2026-08-06.
Reproduce with `.venv/bin/python analysis/kp040.py`; every number below comes
from a CSV in `analysis/out/`.

---

## The answer

**The number is not supported by any data on disk, and it is not contradicted
either — because no run has ever gone there.** Everything that *is* measurable
says the linear stability limit lies far beyond −0.040, and that railing is set
by amplitude rather than by gain. What the data cannot do is rule out a
nonlinear mechanism at −0.040, because nothing has ever driven a coil that hard.

Three findings weaken the number well beyond "unevidenced":

1. It has never been applied.
2. There is no cliff at −0.040 in anything that was measured.
3. It cannot be a single global number anyway — the four channels differ by 4×
   in coil gain, so one Kp means four different loop gains.

`CLAUDE.md`'s "do not exceed −0.035 unattended" remains the right operating
rule. It is just not the rule the docstring claims it is.

---

## 1. `-0.040` has never been commanded on hardware

`gain_census.csv`, over 21 runs with the loop closed:

| ch | runs | max abs gain applied | median | CAPTURE_GAIN |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 21 | **0.035** | 0.030 | 0.035 |
| 1 | 21 | **0.035** | 0.030 | 0.035 |
| 2 | 21 | **0.012** | 0.010 | 0.012 |
| 3 | 21 | **0.035** | 0.030 | 0.035 |

The maximum ever reached is exactly `CAPTURE_GAIN`, on every channel. The gain
schedule is clamped there by construction, so **no logged sample exists at
|Kp| > 0.035.** −0.040 is outside the data entirely, not merely under-sampled.

(ch4–7 show 0.015–0.018 across 3 runs — that is the v5.5 eight-channel attempt,
driving coils against unwired sensors. Not usable evidence.)

## 2. Railing is an amplitude phenomenon, not a gain threshold

The obvious confound: higher gain is *applied* at higher amplitude by design, so
a naive gain-vs-railing correlation measures the schedule, not the physics.
`rail_vs_gain.csv` bins by amplitude first, then compares gains **within** each
bin:

| amplitude bin | gain 0.010 | gain 0.030 | gain 0.035 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 – 0.012 | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0.26% |
| 0.012 – 0.041 | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0.41% |
| 0.041 – 0.270 | 0.0% | 0.01% | 0.79% |
| 0.270 – 0.973 | 0.0% | **9.49%** | — |

Gain does increase railing at fixed amplitude — but amplitude dominates by two
orders of magnitude. `clip_vs_vel.csv` makes the same point directly: across
every run with railed samples, mean |velocity| is **7.4–12.4** on railed samples
against **0.51–0.85** on clean ones. Railing happens at roughly **15× the
typical velocity**, i.e. it is what a large disturbance does, not what a large
gain does.

Extrapolating the recorded velocities through `rail_velocity = 0.25/|Kp|`
(`saturation_vs_kp.csv`) gives a completely smooth curve — **no discontinuity at
−0.040**:

| Kp | −0.020 | −0.030 | −0.035 | **−0.040** | −0.050 | −0.100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| % of damping samples saturating | 0.41 | 0.96 | 1.23 | **1.47** | 1.92 | 3.90 |

If −0.040 were a rail onset, something would happen there. Nothing does.

## 3. Damping increases monotonically across the whole measured range

`damping_vs_gain.csv` — envelope decay rate γ (s⁻¹) from windows of constant
gain, so the gain slew is excluded:

| ch | γ at gain 0 | γ at steady gain | τ: 0 → steady | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.149 | **0.255** (0.030) | 6.72 s → 3.93 s | 27 / 56 |
| 2 | 0.166 | **0.284** (0.010) | 6.03 s → 3.53 s | 14 / 96 |
| 3 | 0.162 | **0.261** (0.030) | 6.16 s → 3.83 s | 32 / 77 |
| 0 | — | 0.191 (0.030) | — → 5.23 s | 46 |

No turnover anywhere in the range hardware has visited. ch2 gets the **largest**
decay rate from the **smallest** gain (0.010), which is the coil-gain story in
§5 and independently re-confirms that its `+0.010` is correct.

## 4. Phase margin is nowhere near exhausted

`phase_budget.csv`, from the controller's own filter chain (0.4 Hz highpass,
3.0 Hz lowpass, derivative + 5 Hz smoother, D-path 2 Hz smoother) plus the
measured loop rate and its zero-order hold:

| config | loop rate | PM @ 1.05 Hz | PM @ 1.66 Hz | PM @ 3.75 Hz |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| v0, P-only, 1 coil | 73 Hz | 164.6° | 139.3° | 88.4° |
| v3+, PID, 1 coil | 73 Hz | 167.0° | 146.5° | 95.0° |
| **v3+, PID, 4 coils** | **23.5 Hz** | **155.4°** | **130.4°** | **73.5°** |
| v5.5, PID, 8 coils | 12.5 Hz | 141.0° | 110.8° | **45.5°** |

Phase only reaches −180° at Nyquist, where loop gain is negligible. At the modal
frequencies the margins are enormous. **A linear phase-margin instability at
−0.040 is not consistent with the loop as built** — it would need ~4× the gain
even to approach the classical limit, which matches the simulator sweeping to
−0.600 without trouble.

## 5. The limit is a loop gain, not a Kp

`per_channel_limit.csv`, using the measured per-coil DC gains:

| ch | coil gain (counts/V) | steady Kp | loop product | equivalent Kp at ch0's limit | running at |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | −105 | −0.030 | 3.15 | −0.040 | **75%** |
| 1 | −68 | −0.030 | 2.04 | −0.062 | 49% |
| 2 | **+213** | +0.010 | 2.13 | +0.020 | 51% |
| 3 | −51 | −0.030 | 1.53 | −0.082 | 36% |

The coil gains span **4×**. If −0.040 was ever a real observation it was an
observation about **ch0** — the only channel that had ever been driven before
2026-08-04. Applied globally it is over-conservative on ch1 and ch3 by 1.5–2×.
ch2 already sits at half its equivalent limit despite the small-looking `+0.010`.

## 6. The loop rate collapses with coil count — and v0 ran 3× faster

`loop_rate.csv`, median inter-sample time with the loop closed:

| coils driven | median dt | loop rate |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 13.7 ms | **73 Hz** |
| 4 | 42.6 ms | **23.5 Hz** |
| 8 | 79.9 ms | **12.5 Hz** |

Each `SET` blocks reading its serial ack, so the loop rate is set by coil count,
not by the 348 Hz stream. This matters for the provenance question: **v0, where
−0.040 was written down, drove ONE coil at 73 Hz. Today's four-channel
controllers run at 23.5 Hz** — 3× slower, with correspondingly less phase margin
(§4). Whatever was seen on v0 does not transfer unchanged to the current
configuration, and if anything today's configuration is the less stable one.

## 7. Two modes, from the sensor spectra alone

`modes.csv` — dominant resonance per channel:

- **1.046 Hz**: ch0, ch2
- **1.657 Hz**: ch1, ch3

Each pair peaks at one frequency and shows the other as a secondary. That is an
independent confirmation, from passive sensor spectra with no actuation at all,
of the "four coils drive two DOF" result that `osem.v6.py`'s rank check gave
(singular values 1.000 / 0.154 / 0.058 / 0.043). Two methods, no shared
assumptions, same answer.

## What is NOT established

- **Anything at |Kp| > 0.035.** No data exists there. A nonlinear mechanism —
  coil driver compression, magnetic nonlinearity, the shadow sensor leaving its
  linear region — could exist at −0.040 and would be invisible here. §4 rules
  out a *linear phase-margin* instability; it rules out nothing else.
- **`spectral_shape.csv` is UNVERIFIED.** It suggests closing the loop raises
  4–8 Hz content by ~300× over open loop (0.0015 → 0.41–0.52 relative power),
  which would be a real candidate mechanism — high-frequency noise injection
  from a coarse update rate rather than a classical instability. But the
  analysis decimates without an anti-alias filter, so that band may be folded
  HF noise. **Do not cite these numbers until the decimation is fixed.**
- **Which channel −0.040 referred to**, and at what loop rate. §5 and §6 say it
  can only have been ch0 at 73 Hz, but that is inference, not a record.

## What would settle it

A gain ramp on **one** channel past −0.035, on the bench, with the raw stream on
disk — the standing practice. §5 says do it on **ch3** first: its coil gain is
the weakest at −51 counts/V, so it has ~2× the margin of ch0 and is the safest
place to look for the onset. Watch for oscillation **above** 1.66 Hz; a
phase-margin instability appears near the −180° crossover, not at the pendulum
resonance, so the frequency of whatever appears is itself diagnostic.
